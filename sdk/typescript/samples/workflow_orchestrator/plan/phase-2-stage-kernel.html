Phase 2 · Stage kernel split

The original Stage Kernel prototype proved the product path but grew to 2,020 additions across 24 files. The first trace-only rebuild then timed out three times after growing past 1,000 tracked additions. Rebuild from the accepted Phase 1 tree as six independently reviewed PR slices.

Executable slices

Slice Single outcome Hard boundary
2A · Trace substrate Four slices: pure replay, prerequisite state, synced writer, then recovery. codex-rollout-trace; no worker lifecycle or runtime bounds.
2B · Workflow lifecycle Terminal-before-cleanup behavior and durable nested parentage. codex-core integration; consume 2A without changing its schema.
2C · Runtime bounds Every host-owned code-mode resource bound is enforced and proven. code-mode; no recovery, parentage, or TypeScript authoring.

Prototype evidence retained

  • workflow-runtime-02: cleanup could wait forever, trace writes were best-effort, and restart identity was not live.
  • workflow-runtime-03: default trace provisioning and parentage were absent.
  • workflow-runtime-04: child reload could recover live siblings, parentage was not durable on resume, stage_id was unbounded, and live/bounds proof was incomplete.
  • workflow-runtime-05: three implementation attempts exhausted the bounded recovery budget; the runner replayed the failure without launching a fourth session. The candidate tree exceeded the intended review boundary and is rejected.
  • workflow-runtime-06: 2A.1 passed both gates. The repaired 2A.2 behavior was coherent, but its 1,160-line delta failed the explicit size gate and was split again.

Each slice receives its own anchored baseline, bounded implementation/repair budget, journey review, and code-quality review. A later slice cannot make an earlier slice pass.

Stop: do not resume or weaken the rejected prototype runs. Use them only as evidence while rebuilding from the accepted Phase 1 tree.
